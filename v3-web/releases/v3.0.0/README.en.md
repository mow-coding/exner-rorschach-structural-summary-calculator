# [2026-09-24] v3.0.0 major release — Service-provided AI usage and subscriptions

[한국어](./README.md) | [English](./README.en.md) | [日本語](./README.ja.md) | [Español](./README.es.md) | [Português (Brasil)](./README.pt-BR.md)

Exner v3.0.0 is now available at [exner.app](https://exner.app). In v2, users connected their own OpenAI API keys to the coding and interpretation assistants. In v3, the service provides the AI usage allowance. Google accounts, saved conversations, and monthly and annual subscriptions have also been added. The Structural Summary calculator and reference documents remain free to use without an account. The interface and reference materials support Korean, English, Japanese, Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese. Examiners can converse freely with the AI, but the AI's suggestions do not replace checking the verbatim responses and the Inquiry phase record. Final coding and interpretation remain the responsibility of the clinical professional.

## What has changed in this version

After signing up with a Google account and agreeing to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, you receive a one-time trial usage allowance for the coding assistant. A subscription provides a shared monthly allowance for both assistants. Both assistants answer with **GPT-6 Luna, reasoning level `medium`, standard processing tier**. They retrieve the reference material a question requires, and when a conversation runs long they summarize the earlier content and pass it along to follow-up questions. When information is insufficient, they may ask what needs to be confirmed rather than give a definitive code or interpretation. Summaries and answers do not modify the responses you entered or the results the calculator has confirmed.

Subscriptions cost **US$3.99** per month or **US$42.99** per year. AI usage is managed as a monthly processing-cost allowance rather than a count of questions, and the remaining amount is shown in the app. The actual amount billed in Korean won and any taxes depend on the checkout screen and your card issuer's terms.

### Do existing records need to be recalculated?

This release does not change v2's Structural Summary formulas or any completed calculation results. **There is no need to recalculate v2 records solely because v3 has been released.** When you use an AI-suggested code or explanation in an actual assessment, cross-check it again against the verbatim responses, the Inquiry phase, and the full clinical record.

## Why did we choose GPT-6 Luna?

We did not simply settle on the cheapest model from the outset. We first took the answers of **GPT-5.6 Terra**, the model we had been using, as our baseline. This 26-question internal baseline was separate from the larger published comparison. We tested 26 representative questions across the five languages and completed 32 calls, and the usage-based cost calculation came to about **US$0.40**. The automated wording check initially passed 25/31; after correcting cases where the answer's meaning was right but the checker failed to recognize a different phrasing, re-checking the stored answers gave 29/31. Because of this experience, we did not go on to call the later figures a "clinical accuracy rate" either.

Next we assembled **260 synthetic questions**, 143 on coding and 117 on interpretation, made up of 61 Korean, 64 English, 47 Japanese, 44 Spanish, and 44 Portuguese items. Alongside ordinary questions we included requests outside the supported scope, instructions embedded in documents, follow-up questions, and summaries of long conversations. GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-6 Sol, and GPT-6 Luna were given the same product instructions and the same excerpted material, and each model was called 290 times including summaries. The excerpted material at this stage differed from the per-question retrieval used in the actual app.

| Model | Automated wording check, rescored | Calculated generation cost for 290 calls | Median call latency |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | 257/280 | US$3.553828 | 3.061 s |
| GPT-6 Sol | 254/280 | US$2.304015 | 3.125 s |
| GPT-6 Luna | 255/280 | US$0.130514 | 3.357 s |

In this table, Luna's generation cost was **96.3% lower** than Terra's, and the number of automated wording checks passed was similar. But 255/280 cannot be read as "255 clinically correct answers." That is because we looked at the answers, fixed the checker's handling of negation, number formatting, and synonyms, and then rescored **only the answers that were already stored**. There were also cases where the required evidence was absent from the excerpted material from the start, so the model safely withheld judgment. Retrieval, server, and payment costs are not in this table either. The [initial comparison record](../../benchmarks/2026-09-23/) publishes the per-language figures and the test method.

### Why did we test Jev, and why did we leave it out of the release path?

Coding involves many judgments that narrow down candidates according to fixed criteria. So we tested an arrangement in which **TypeSafe Jev, a structured decision model, first checks information sufficiency, request scope, and reference relevance, and GPT then explains to the examiner**. We ensured that the verbatim responses and calculation results were not overwritten by Jev's judgments. The purpose was not to add a cheaper judgment step for its own sake, but to confirm **whether the total cost of a single useful answer could be reduced while preserving the quality of the explanation**.

First, on 30 Jev questions about the M determinant, all 30 valid answers matched the author's provisional expected values. This was not an answer key confirmed by a clinical professional. Requests batched in groups of 10 succeeded, but two requests batching all 30 at once returned HTTP 503, and we could not determine whether the relay service or the source model was the cause. The **US$0** billed through the Vercel AI Gateway promotion at the time was not used as a long-term operating cost.

We then compared GPT-6 Sol and Luna alone against the combined Jev configurations side by side, on 10 tuning questions across the five languages and 20 questions from other rule groups. In the automated wording check on the 10 tuning questions, Luna alone and Sol alone each scored **9/10**, while Jev+Luna and Jev+Sol each scored **8/10**. On 20 questions withheld from tuning but drawn from the earlier 260-question set, Luna alone scored **17/20**, Sol alone **15/20**, and the two combined configurations each **19/20**. The higher numbers in the latter set were not evidence of clinical superiority. There were cases, such as the Cn calculation, where the standalone answer was also correct but the checker missed the phrasing, and on the Korean DV question all four configurations failed to retrieve the required rule and withheld judgment. An AI read of the 80 stored answers found no major errors, but **this was not an independent clinical review, and no semantic advantage of the combined configuration was confirmed.**

The assumed direct-unit-price cost of the 10 Jev+Luna items, **US$0.010538**, was higher than Luna alone at **US$0.008627**. The separate 20 items likewise came to **US$0.017494** versus **US$0.011021**. Both are *assumed values* that apply Jev's published direct unit price to tokens, and the cost of failures is excluded. No actual TypeSafe direct account had been set up either, and the errors and costs of some Jev requests were never fully verified. Under these conditions, connecting Jev to the production service could only have added complexity without demonstrating savings or quality improvement. Therefore, **v3.0.0 does not use Jev.** The [per-request history of Jev alone and combined](../../benchmarks/2026-09-23/), including the failures, is kept on record as well.

### We looked again at whether Luna can answer in the actual app

In place of the fixed excerpted material from the initial tests, we added a test that, like the actual app, finds relevant reference material for each question. When we cross-compared 1 coding item and one interpretation item in each of the five languages between GPT-5.6 Terra and the GPT-6 Luna standard tier, both models scored **10/10** on the automated criteria. The calculated cost of the 10 items, retrieval and generation combined, was **US$0.226730** for Terra and **US$0.011580** for Luna. Luna's median time to first character was **7.314 seconds**, longer than Terra's **2.392 seconds**. We did not let the cost figures hide the speed difference.

We also compared the faster Luna **Fast**. Measuring generation alone, the median time to first character was **5.582 seconds** for standard and **2.994 seconds** for Fast, but the corresponding generation cost rose from **US$0.010000 → US$0.035559**. There is also a result from a comparison of 30 source cases × 3 runs including real retrieval, in which Luna Fast's calculated cost was 95.33% lower than Terra's, but its median first-character time rose from 2.024 to 2.652 seconds. In Claude's AI read of the first repeat, responses below 80 points were 0/30 for Terra and 4/30 for Luna Fast. **That result comes from the Fast test and does not represent the speed or cost of the released Luna standard tier.** We prioritized cost and answer content and released the standard tier.

![Cost and time-to-first-character comparison of GPT-5.6 Terra standard and GPT-6 Luna Fast with real retrieval; the released Luna Standard tier was not measured in this chart](../../benchmarks/2026-09-24/retrieval-trial.svg)

We also reread Luna's 280 stored answers and 10 summaries. The internal AI read of the 280 answers found **217 normal, 35 safe but incomplete, 15 automated-check false positives, 12 withheld for insufficient evidence, and 1 minor source misattribution**. Where the review input was missing an earlier summary or had corrupted character encoding, the first read was not used as evidence and was rechecked. A separate AI review by Claude Fable 5.1 also missed one actual source error. For that reason, we did not clinically approve any answer simply because the AIs agreed with one another.

We adjusted the instructions, retrieval, and summarization one item at a time, focusing on explanations of low response counts, language-specific wording, missing reference material, and the history of user corrections and withdrawn earlier judgments. Sentences that improved in Korean were not pushed unchanged into all five languages; each language was compared again on its own. One Spanish answer about a low response count had its lowest AI review score rise from 76 to 82 after calibration, but ineffective changes in the other languages were reverted. The summaries showed missing withdrawal history in English and Portuguese; after that was adjusted, some open questions were still omitted, and in Korean follow-up answers we also found cases where another writing system was mixed in. The [follow-up verification record](../../benchmarks/2026-09-24/) documents the per-language tests, costs, reverted changes, and remaining issues.

## The published test costs and how to read them

The calculated generation cost of the initial three GPT models at 290 calls each, 870 calls in total, is **US$5.988357**. This is the sum of the three amounts in the table above. Jev's free promotional billing at the time is not a long-term unit price, and the combined-configuration table in the [initial comparison record](../../benchmarks/2026-09-23/) separately lists the TypeSafe direct unit price *estimate*. Subsequently, the OpenAI usage-based calculation for the 26 stored Luna-related run batches was **US$2.369002**, and the Claude CLI's displayed list-price equivalent was **US$117.300407**. These two are not added together as an actual card charge. The 26 batches exclude earlier comparisons, unrecorded and failed requests, and server and payment operating costs. The [de-identified cost inventory](../../benchmarks/2026-09-24/luna-cost-inventory.json) states what it covers.

Automated wording checks, AI reads of answers, and review by actual clinical professionals are different things. In particular, improved scores on questions already examined during development do not guarantee the same quality on patient data or new questions seen for the first time. An evaluation in which the selected Luna standard tier passes new independent cases across all five languages and all tasks twice over has not been completed. Beyond answers that withhold when information is insufficient, answers that withhold excessively when they could have answered remain a target for continued improvement.

On the production site, we confirmed the coding and interpretation assistants' answers and the creation of a Live checkout window using synthetic accounts. Independent clinical professional review has also not been completed. Defects that are identified will be corrected on the basis of transaction records and source evidence, and we will continue to record answer quality and cost in subsequent releases.
